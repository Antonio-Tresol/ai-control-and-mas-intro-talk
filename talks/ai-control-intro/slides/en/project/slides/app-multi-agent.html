An attack split across agents can look benign in every single transcript, so the monitor has to review agents together: trajectories side by side, the channels they talk over, or their activations. SCHEME (Radev et al., arXiv 2605.29178, May 2026): 17 task instances built so that no subset of agents can finish the covert side task alone. A GPT 5.1 Codex Mini monitor at FPR ≤ 2% flags 68% of GPT 5.1 Codex's coordinated sabotage from code edits alone and 81% when it also reads agent messages (99% and 100% for Gemini 3.1 Pro). Scoring each agent's slice separately loses 20 points of TPR on Codex. The authors note it is not a full control evaluation, and steganography would bypass channel monitoring. NARCBench (Rose et al., arXiv 2604.01151, Apr 2026): probes that combine per-agent activation scores into a group verdict reach 1.00 AUROC in distribution and 0.73–0.93 under transfer, but only 0.38 recall at 1% FPR on transfer. Main results use 4 agents. FakeLab (Makins et al., arXiv 2607.07368, Jul 2026): a joint monitor that reads all commits squashed together catches distributed attacks (mean suspicion 0.91), but the defender does not know which commits belong together; the authors name detecting and incriminating distributed attacks as future work. MATS Summer 2026 talk (Kitaoka & Pham; YouTube auto-captions): joint review recovers much of the signal, but not all. Hammond et al. (arXiv 2502.14143, Feb 2025): calls for monitoring networks of agents as a whole and for general collusion-detection methods.
