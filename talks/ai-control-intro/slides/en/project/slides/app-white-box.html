A1

White-box monitoring gives the monitor the model's internal activations, read through probes or through tools that turn activations into text, so it can flag intent that never appears in the output or the chain of thought. All three readouts in the drawing come from interpretability research. NLA: natural language autoencoders, two copies of the model trained with RL, one mapping an activation to text and one mapping the text back (Fraser-Taliente et al., Anthropic, 7 May 2026, transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla). Used in Claude Opus 4.6's pre-deployment audit. The 16%: on a destructive-coding evaluation, NLA explanations show signs of evaluation awareness 16% of the time although Claude never verbalizes it (Anthropic research post, 7 May 2026). The paper treats NLA-measured awareness as a relative metric and notes that explanations can confabulate. Oracle: activation oracles, LLMs fine-tuned to answer natural-language questions about another model's activations (Karvonen et al., arXiv 2512.15674, Dec 2025). They recover fine-tuned knowledge that never appears in the input; the authors call them non-mechanistic and expensive at inference. J-lens: the Jacobian lens, a refinement of the logit lens that reads which words an activation is poised to make the model say; what it reads is the J-space (Gurnee et al., Anthropic, arXiv 2607.15495, Jul 2026). The 4 of 6: on an automated auditing benchmark the J-lens auditor performed comparably to, or slightly better than, the NLA auditor on four of six cases. The authors do not claim that monitoring the J-space is sufficient. Monitoring: a MATS Summer 2026 talk (Xing & Lin, mentor Tomek Korbak; YouTube auto-captions) found NLA and J-space sometimes good for monitoring, with chain of thought still best, and named choosing which tokens to verbalize as the challenge. Probes can be evaded: models RL-trained against token-level probes learn to evade them, while max-pooling and attention probes held up (Gupta & Jenner, arXiv 2506.14261).
