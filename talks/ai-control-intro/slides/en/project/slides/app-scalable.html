A3

Monitoring has to keep up as agent usage grows by orders of magnitude while audit budgets and latency tolerance stay fixed. Cascades: run a cheap monitor on everything and a stronger one only on its hits (Apollo Research agenda, 8 May 2026). Claude Code auto mode does this in production (Anthropic Engineering, 25 Mar 2026): a fast single-token filter, then chain-of-thought review only on flags. False positives on real traffic fall from 8.5% to 0.4%; misses on real overeager actions rise from 6.6% to 17% (n = 52). Hua et al. (arXiv 2507.15886): choosing when and which monitors to call more than doubles recall over a naive baseline in a code-review setting, and two monitors combined can beat either alone. Latency: Lindner et al. (arXiv 2512.22154, Dec 2025) model 1,000 parallel agents, a 1% FPR monitor and 1-hour audits: fully synchronous monitoring leaves about 1% availability, semi-synchronous 91%, asynchronous 100%; the non-blocking forms fail when agents coordinate simultaneous, redundant attacks. Async Control (Cooper Stickland et al., UK AISI, arXiv 2512.13526): an ensemble monitor reaches a 6% false negative rate at 1% FPR on a held-out environment, and synchronous protocols still give higher safety. Capability gap: Engels et al. (arXiv 2504.18530) fit scaling laws for oversight; at a 400 Elo gap, nested oversight succeeds 10.0% of the time on Backdoor Code and 51.7% on Debate. Open, as the sources name it: control environments with checkable ground truth are not yet scalable enough on their own (Apollo).
