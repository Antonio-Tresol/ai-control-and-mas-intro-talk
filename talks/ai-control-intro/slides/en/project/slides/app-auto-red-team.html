A7

Automated red teaming uses AI agents and optimization loops to generate attacks against monitors and protocols, so the red team scales past what people write by hand. Terekhov et al. (arXiv 2510.09462, ICLR 2026): prompt injections adapted to the monitor make a GPT-4.1 monitor score all 375 backdoored GPT-5 APPS samples at 0; protocol safety falls to that of upfront auditing, and Defer-to-Resample amplifies the injection. Loughridge et al. (arXiv 2511.02823, Nov 2025): an attack scaffold tuned in a cheap simulation of SHADE-Arena cuts safety from 0.87 to 0.41 (Claude 3.7 Sonnet attacker, Claude 3.5 Sonnet monitor, 0.5% FPR). MonitoringBench (Jotautaitė et al., arXiv 2605.09684, May 2026): refined attacks cut an Opus 4.5 monitor's catch rate from 94.9% to 60.3%, and attacks tuned on three monitors generalize to ten held-out ones. UK AISI Control Red Team (blog, 23 Jul 2026): an evolutionary search over attack transcripts reaches a minimum suspicion of 3/100 in all three runs, against best-of-N scores of 14, 15 and 18. Remedios et al. (arXiv 2609.19587, Sep 2026): given high-level strategies, an attacking agent ran arbitrary commands past Claude Code auto mode and Codex Guardian through injections in 79% of trials; a human working with an agent found more than the agent alone, and the improved monitor cut attack success by over 50 points. Open, as the sources name it: how strong and realistic a simulated attacker should be (UK AISI), and whether iterated red-blue play compounds in the monitor's favour (Apollo Research agenda, 8 May 2026).
