A5

Sting operations test an untrusted AI with situations built to look like a real chance to misbehave. Honeypots on single models are studied. Ctrl-Z (Bhatt et al., arXiv 2504.10374, Apr 2025) validates an untrusted monitor on honeypots before deployment; an honest untrusted monitor catches 98.8% of attacks at a 1% auditing budget, and the red team answers with collusion signals such as calling uname, lscpu and pwd. Shlegeris (Redwood, 25 Sep 2024) explains why synthetic attacks test monitors well: a monitor cannot rely on failing rarely, because reporting one real attack gets the policy caught. What's worse, spies or schemers? (Shlegeris & Stastny, Redwood, 9 Jul 2025): sting operations are probably easier on schemers than on human spies, because a schemer's memory can be erased and its information controlled; schemers might defeat schemer–schemer stings by coordinating on shared Schelling points. Planted agents: Idowu et al. (arXiv 2601.00360, Jan 2026) propose whistleblower agents inside multi-agent systems (peer, shadow and adversarial), rewarded for reporting collusion. The paper is conceptual, with no experiments, and lists open problems: false reports, gaming of the leniency scheme, retaliation against defectors. Hammond et al. (arXiv 2502.14143, §3.3) recommend honeypots in multi-agent test environments.
