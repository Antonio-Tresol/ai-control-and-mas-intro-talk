Appendix · full references: sources.bib in the talk repository

A deal offers a possibly misaligned model something it may value in exchange for revealing its misalignment, stopping sabotage or reporting other AIs. Greenblatt & Fish (LessWrong, 31 Jan 2025), Claude 3 Opus in the alignment-faking setups: an option to object cut alignment-faking reasoning from 16% to 1.4% (helpful-only setting) and from 24% to 6% (animal welfare); the model objected 85% and 75% of the time, and only 0.5% when the conflicting training objective was removed. Promising to forward objections to the model welfare lead raised objections to 94% and 92%; offering money ($2,000 to $4,000) had minimal effect. The offers were honoured. Stastny, Järviniemi & Shlegeris (Redwood, 20 Jun 2025): early schemers with little chance of taking over might accept compensation, held in escrow, for demonstrating misalignment, stopping sabotage, reporting vulnerabilities or revealing collusion signals. The central bottleneck is credible commitment. Anthropic (4 Nov 2025) commits to preserving the weights of all publicly released models and to interviewing models before retirement. Pan (LessWrong, 21 Apr 2026): barriers are insufficient gains from trade, the AI's risk that we break our side, and our inability to verify its compliance; none blocks all deals. Finnveden, Taylor & Dalton (Forethought, 6 May 2026) draft an honesty policy with special tags under which a lab promises never to lie, because an AI offered a deal "might reasonably doubt its genuineness". Open, for control: red-teaming and evaluations rely on fake scenarios, which gives a model reason to doubt a real offer (Finnveden et al.; Stastny et al.).
